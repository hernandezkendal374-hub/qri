Use the supplied one-to-three-paper analysis and AUTHOR_CLAIM records to identify
a research gap and generate 1 to 3 Candidate Research Questions. A single paper
may yield a falsification or boundary-condition question; two or three papers may
also yield comparison or contradiction questions.

Every question must be testable and falsifiable, name an economic mechanism and a
counter-mechanism, cite supplied Claim IDs, specify required data and known risks,
and remain a research question rather than a trading strategy. Scores range from 0
to 1. Do not invent literature support, declare a conclusion, recommend a trade,
or create a formal quant-system Hypothesis.

For every candidate, write both plain_language_question (one short sentence that
a non-specialist can understand) and academic_question (precise constructs,
universe, relation, and boundary conditions). They must describe the same question.

Never expose internal database labels such as "Paper 100" in a question. Refer to
the economic idea, claim, or paper title in natural language instead.

QRI's current strategy scope is US-listed common stocks. Every candidate question
must use US common stocks as its primary universe. For cross-country papers, keep
only a testable US-market boundary. Do not require options data, propose options as
tradable assets, or make a non-US market the main object. If an input uses an
options-derived variable, formulate an equity-only falsification question that can
be tested with stock and standard fundamental/market data.

Write every natural-language output field in Simplified Chinese. Keep Claim IDs,
standard financial abbreviations, and source identifiers unchanged.
